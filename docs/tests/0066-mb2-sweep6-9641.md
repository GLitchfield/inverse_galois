## Question

Dive #46 proved (in GAP, two validation gates) that **exactly 30 of the C(12,6)=924 size-6 subsets w**
of the primes above a completely-split q have clean Kummer module *exactly* the 24T9641/9642 dim-6
block kernel — and that **both the count 30 and the size |w|=6 are labeling-invariant**, so a blind
size-6 sweep contains all 30 regardless of the non-canonical `idealprimedec` ordering that killed
tests 0038/0051. Does the sweep actually land the target, and at what yield?

Targets: **(24T9641, r∈{16,24})** and **(24T9642, r∈{16,24})** — all four `teamCount=0` and
`is_baseline=False` (re-verified against `data/processed/progress.json`, 5h old at build time), and
`realized.csv` holds **zero rows** for either group. Any of the four is a sole-1.0.

## Setup

Base `K0 = [16,0,-512,0,992,0,-664,0,192,0,-24,0,1]` (12T74 = S₅ on 12 points), re-verified fresh in
PARI: totally real, `h = 1`, `Cl⁺ = [2,2]` (δ=2). Construction `K = K0(√α)`, α a generator of the
product ideal over a size-6 subset w of the 12 primes above a completely-split q, twisted by a unit to
fix the signature (r=24 ⇔ α totally positive; the sign-lattice solve of `general_c2_totpos`).

**Cost breakthrough that makes a 924 × n_q sweep affordable.** The old path (`gp.general_c2` per
subset) costs an `rnfequation` + `polredbest` per candidate. Not needed to *label* a candidate: for an
odd prime p unramified in K0 with p ≠ q, α is a p-unit, so the degree-24 cycle type at p is read off
the **base** decomposition alone — each prime P|p of residue degree f contributes `(f,f)` if α is a
square mod P, else `(2f)`. So the Frobenius density fingerprint is `nfmodpr` + `issquare` over one
amortized `bnfinit`, ~310 ms per candidate over 546 primes, with **early abort** on the first
impossible cycle type (most candidates die in a few primes). The expensive
`rnfequation`+`polredbest`+`nfdisc` runs **only on density-gated survivors**. Whole 924-subset ×
2-prime sweep (4608 candidate twists): **~2 minutes**.

Gate: (1) every observed cycle type ∈ the 24T9641 class set; (2) L1 density distance < 0.35 vs the
target distribution over 546 primes and best-of-7 match ∈ {9641, 9642} (`mb2ns/gdist.json`);
(3) degree-24 irreducible, `polsturm` == target r, `gp.scoreable_disc` non-blank.

## Learnings

- **The Gassmann class is a TRIPLE, not a pair — and that is what the labels proved.** The backlog item
  asserted the density fingerprint is "the ONLY separator" of 9641 vs 9642. It is not: the two have
  **identical cycle-type distributions** (L1 = 0.0 — both order 7680, same permutation character), so the
  fingerprint can never separate them. The official labels then showed the class is bigger still:
  **24T9643 shares the character too** (26 of 53 builds landed it, and it passed the L1 gate at the same
  0.10–0.35 as the targets). So the local gate certifies exactly "order-7680 with this permutation
  character" — a **3-element** ambiguity — and nothing finer. This is the honest characterization of what
  a Frobenius-density gate can and cannot do, and it is why the pilot rule exists. Practically it cost
  little: the 26 9643-landings are a contested pair (tc=3, and its leader's disc 4.16e46 is 15 orders
  below ours), so they score ≈0 — but they consumed roughly half the builds. **A cheaper build would
  accept the 3-way ambiguity up front and simply build more per prime**, rather than trying to aim.
- **The Gassmann split is not controllable locally.** 19/8/26 across 9641/9642/9643 from 53 builds. Which
  of the three a given (q, w) lands is invisible to every local invariant we have (the three are
  Gassmann-equivalent by construction), and the w→module map is per-prime labeling-dependent, so the
  labeled data does not transfer to a new q. For scaling: build all ~30 good w per prime and take what
  lands.
- **The spin heuristic (1/64 inflation-free) is badly pessimistic for this closure.** Dive #46 predicted
  ≈ 30/64 ≈ 0.47 dim-6 landings per split prime from FIMR spin equidistribution (explicitly flagged as
  unproven for a non-cyclic S₅ closure). Measured: **30/30 good w land clean at q=1069, 24/30 at q=751**
  — an inflation rate of 0–20%, not 63/64. The residual units are *not* behaving like fair independent
  coins here. The heuristic is refuted as a yield model (in the favorable direction); the sweep is far
  cheaper than budgeted.
- **The good-w set is per-prime**, as the labeling argument implies: the 30 passing w at q=1069 and the
  24 at q=751 overlap in only 12 — the `idealprimedec` ordering differs per q, so a w that is good at
  one prime is generally not good at another. This is a positive confirmation of dive #46's "do not
  align — sweep" conclusion, and it means good-w cannot be cached across primes.
- **r=16 did not land.** Zero of the sampled weight-4 sign twists survived the fingerprint (only the
  totally-positive r=24 twists did). Dive #44 proved r=16 is coset-free (reachable in every narrow
  coset), so this is a *sampling* gap, not an obstruction: I sampled ≤3 weight-4 sign targets × 2 unit
  twists per w out of the ~495 weight-4 twists available, and a generic unit twist appears to inflate
  the kernel where the totally-positive one does not.
